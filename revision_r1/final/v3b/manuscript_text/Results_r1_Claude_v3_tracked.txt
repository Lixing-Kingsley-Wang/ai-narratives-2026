Results
Corpus and Overall Stance
Of 86,700 eligible Q1/Q2 records published from 2021 to 2025, the prefilter retained 14,066 AI-engaged records: 7637 discourse and 6429 evaluative (Figure 1). Stance classification failed for 8 records, leaving 14,058 records in the analytic corpus. The annual keep rate rose from 9.5% (1064/11,191) in 2021 to 20.4% (5899/28,873) in 2025 ([[MA:keep_rate]]). The audit estimated that 5.2% (95% CI 4.1%-8.9%) of excluded records were AI-engaged, about 3747 records (95% CI 2946-6447). The false-negative rate was 3.3% (95% CI 2.3%-8.2%) in 2021–2022 and 5.7% (95% CI 3.8%-11.4%) in 2024–2025, a difference of 2.5 percentage points (95% CI -2.5 to 7.6) ([[MA:prefilter_validation]]).
Overall, 30.5% of records (4283/14,058; 95% CI 29.7%-31.2%) were critical, comprising 749 Alarm and 3534 Caution records. Cautious Optimism was the majority stance at 62.9% (8836/14,058), with Neutral at 5.1% (721/14,058) and Advocacy rare at 1.6% (218/14,058) (Figure 2A).

Temporal Change in Evaluative Stance
Critical share was 25.4% (270/1063) in 2021 and 24.8% (333/1343) in 2022. It rose to 30.4% (624/2054) in 2023 and 33.2% (1228/3700) in 2024, and was 31.0% (1828/5898) in 2025 (Figure 2A). The odds of a critical stance increased with publication year (OR 1.077 per year, 95% CI 1.038-1.116; P&lt;.001), and the trend remained after adjustment for genre, publication type, specialty, and first-author region (adjusted OR 1.103, 95% CI 1.066-1.141; P&lt;.001; Table 2). Compared with 2021, the adjusted odds were similar in 2022 (OR 1.00, 95% CI 0.83-1.21) and higher in 2023 (OR 1.31, 95% CI 1.09-1.56), 2024 (OR 1.53, 95% CI 1.30-1.80), and 2025 (OR 1.45, 95% CI 1.23-1.70) ([[MA:regression_full]]). Critical share therefore rose after 2022 and remained elevated. Alarm rose from 2.7% in 2021 to 6.8% in 2024 and was 5.7% in 2025; Caution rose from 22.7% to 25.3%. Cautious Optimism remained the majority every year, from 60.2% to 65.3% ([[MA:stance_trend]]).
The trend was similar when records were compared within the same journal (OR 1.082 per year, 95% CI 1.030-1.137), in the SJR Medicine corpus (OR 1.066, 95% CI 1.029-1.106), and after the records missed by the prefilter were added (OR 1.092, 95% CI 1.055-1.130) (Table 3). The added records were less often critical (310/2453, 12.6%), so they lowered critical share from 25.4% to 22.0% in 2021 and from 31.0% to 28.6% in 2025 ([[MA:corpus_sensitivity]]). The level of critical share therefore depended on how the corpus was defined, and its trend was stable across definitions. In the Q3 comparison arm (n=853), critical share was 23.0% in 2021 and 28.1% in 2025 (OR 1.093, 95% CI 0.981-1.218; P=.106; [[MA:q3_arm]]). After correction for misclassification, critical share rose from 26.99% (95% CI 21.82%-32.64%) in 2021 to 31.36% (95% CI 26.25%-36.75%) in 2025, a change of 4.37 percentage points (95% CI 2.61-5.99; [[MA:corrected_prevalence]]).
The rise was present within both prefilter genres. Critical share rose from 30.2% (222/735) to 35.4% (1016/2867) among discourse records (OR 1.061, 95% CI 1.015-1.108) and from 14.6% (48/328) to 26.8% (812/3031) among evaluative records (OR 1.209, 95% CI 1.144-1.277) ([[MA:within_genre_fig]]; [[MA:within_genre]]). Meanwhile, the discourse:evaluative ratio fell from 2.24 in 2021 to 0.95 in 2025. The overall rise was therefore not only due to changing genre composition.
Advocacy fell in every year, from 2.9% (31/1063) in 2021 to 1.1% (66/5898) in 2025 (OR 0.793 per year, 95% CI 0.720-0.873; adjusted OR 0.841, 95% CI 0.754-0.939; Figure 2A). The decline appeared in all three measures of unqualified promotion: the classifier's Advocacy label, a strong-endorsement flag from the symmetric stance scale, and a misclassification-corrected estimate based on human coding ([[MA:advocacy_measures_fig]]). On the symmetric stance scale, which replaced Advocacy with a strong-endorsement category defined to mirror Alarm, strong endorsement fell from 10.8% (115/1063) in 2021 to 6.6% (390/5898) in 2025, after a peak of 13.2% (177/1343) in 2022 (OR 0.834 per year, 95% CI 0.795-0.875; [[MA:symmetric_endorsement]]). Of the 218 Advocacy records, 83 (38.1%) were also classified as strong endorsement. After correction for misclassification, the estimated share of Advocacy as the human coder defined it fell from 6.16% (95% CI 3.13%-9.65%) in 2021 to 5.13% (95% CI 2.33%-8.41%) in 2025, a change of -1.03 percentage points (95% CI -1.79 to -0.42; [[MA:corrected_prevalence]]). The higher corrected level reflects records the classifier labelled Cautious Optimism and the coder labelled Advocacy. Advocacy also fell in the augmented corpus (OR 0.840, 95% CI 0.764-0.923) and in the SJR Medicine corpus (OR 0.771, 95% CI 0.691-0.860) (Table 3).
The decline was broad: the Advocacy rate fell in 11 of the 18 specialty and domain categories, including Medical Informatics/Digital Health, the largest source of Advocacy records, where it fell from 4.1% (12/293) in 2021 to 1.8% (28/1517) in 2025 ([[MA:advocacy_by_specialty]]). Beyond the analytic window, the partial January–April 2026 stratum, reported descriptively, had a critical share of 32.7% (878/2689), close to the 2024–2025 levels, and an Advocacy share of 0.6% (17/2689) ([[MA:update_2026]]).

Concern Themes Within Critical Abstracts
Each of the 4283 critical records was assigned one to three themes. Annual prevalence used the number of critical records in each year as the denominator. Patient safety was the most prevalent theme in every year, at 59.3% (160/270) in 2021 and 64.9% (1187/1828) in 2025, and changed little over time (OR 1.055 per year, 95% CI 0.995-1.118; P=.071) (Figure 2B). Hallucination/errors rose from 23.7% (64/270) to 51.1% (934/1828) (OR 1.367, 95% CI 1.289-1.450) and became the second most prevalent concern from 2023. Governance/regulation fell from 54.8% (148/270) to 31.7% (580/1828) (OR 0.765, 95% CI 0.719-0.814). This was a decline in prevalence share: the number of regulation-themed records rose from 148 to 580 as the critical corpus expanded. Ethics/bias peaked at 45.3% (151/333) in 2022 and was 33.0% (604/1828) in 2025, close to its 2021 level of 36.7% (99/270) (OR 0.915, 95% CI 0.861-0.972). The other six themes are in [[MA:other_themes_fig]] and [[MA:themes]], and counts with prevalence shares are in [[MA:theme_counts_fig]].
As an independent check on the LLM themes, a prespecified lexicon counted critical records whose title, abstract, author keywords, or MeSH descriptors contained any term from a fixed word list for each headline theme. The lexicon moved in the same direction as the LLM themes for hallucination/errors, from 8.1% to 22.6% of critical records, and for patient safety, from 13.7% to 19.2% ([[MA:lexicon_vs_llm_fig]]; [[MA:lexicon_and_terms]]). For governance/regulation and ethics/bias, the lexicon fell with the LLM themes from 2021 to 2024 and rose in 2025. In the text the theme classifier read, the title and the first 400 characters of the abstract, the regulation lexicon was stable across the 5 years (OR 1.000, 95% CI 0.903-1.107). The 2025 rise came from later in the abstract, led by terms such as "oversight" and "accountability"; the phrase "human oversight" appeared in 0.7% (8/1228) of critical abstracts in 2024 and 3.2% (58/1828) in 2025 ([[MA:lexicon_decomposition]]). Among MeSH descriptors and author keywords, "large language models" and "ChatGPT" rose most between 2021–2022 and 2024–2025, whereas "machine learning" and "deep learning" fell most. "Delivery of health care," "ethics," and "bias" were most over-represented in critical records, and "deep learning" in favourable records ([[MA:terms_fig]]).
Failure Mechanisms: Factual Error and Fabrication
Mechanism classification parsed 4281 of the 4283 critical records, and 3906 of these had an abstract. Within the hallucination-gated subset of 1775 abstract-present records, factual error was the majority mechanism in every year, ranging from 56.3% (121/215) in 2023 to 90.2% (55/61) in 2022 (Figure 3A). Fabrication-involved papers, defined as fabrication alone or with factual error, rose from 0% (0/56) in 2021 to 15.8% (34/215) in 2023, and were 23.4% (131/560) in 2024 and 20.6% (182/883) in 2025 (OR 1.347 per year, 95% CI 1.197-1.515). The hallucination theme captured 91.3% (348/381) of fabrication-involved papers, but only 19.6% (348/1775) of gated papers concerned fabrication. The hallucination label therefore did not denote fabrication alone.
Across all 3906 abstract-present critical records, the generative-model share rose from 5.3% (13/245) in 2021 to 57.8% (1015/1757) in 2025 (OR 2.026 per year, 95% CI 1.892-2.170) (Figure 3B). Fabrication share also rose (OR 1.552, 95% CI 1.416-1.701) but stayed in the low teens, at 13.0% (140/1076) in 2024 and 11.4% (201/1757) in 2025. Among generative-model papers, fabrication stayed near 20% from 2023 to 2025 (21.8%, 22.9%, and 19.7%; OR 0.977, 95% CI 0.847-1.125). In total, 1446 records (37.0%) concerned generative models without a fabrication concern, and only 1 paper raised fabrication in a discriminative system. Because failure-mode agreement was moderate and the classifier assigned fabrication more readily than the human coder, fabrication estimates are an upper bound.

Specialty Variation and Cleared-Device Availability
Specialty analyses included 14,048 records with a valid category. Critical rate varied roughly threefold across the 18 categories (Figure 4A). It was highest in Mental Health/Psychiatry at 50.3% (196/390), Medical Informatics/Digital Health at 45.2% (1674/3701), and Nursing at 41.4% (94/227). It was lowest in Cardiology at 16.1% (87/542), Ophthalmology at 18.5% (124/669), and Oncology at 18.7% (227/1211). After adjustment for publication year, genre, publication type, and first-author region, the average marginal probability of a critical stance ranged from 46.7% in Mental Health/Psychiatry to 17.6% in Cardiology (Table 2).
Critical rate was inversely associated with the FDA cleared-device count across the 18 categories (Spearman ρ=-0.61; two-sided P=.007; Figure 4B). Radiology, Cardiology, and Pathology had among the largest mapped device counts and lower critical rates. Excluding the 7 structural-zero categories gave a directionally similar estimate (ρ=-0.58, n=11; P=.062). Controlling for generative share left the association unchanged (partial ρ=-0.61; P=.010), and critical rate was uncorrelated with generative share (ρ=0.01; P=.971) ([[MA:fda_robustness]]; [[MA:generative_share_fig]]). This exploratory ecological association across 18 category-level units does not establish a mechanism.

First-Author Geography
Critical rate varied by first-author country among the 32 countries with at least 50 records (Figure 5; [[MA:country]]). Papers with a United States first author were critical in 34.2% (1226/3586) of cases, compared with 14.9% (230/1540) for China (OR 2.81, 95% CI 2.26-3.48, adjusted for publication year, genre, publication type, and specialty). Rates were 40% or higher in the Netherlands (44.2%), Australia (43.8%), Denmark (41.8%), Ireland (41.4%), and the United Kingdom (40.0%). Rates were near or below 20% in South Korea (19.5%), India (19.7%), Japan (18.3%), and Iran (17.6%).
By region, North America (34.9%, 1472/4221) and Western Europe (34.5%, 1274/3693) had similar rates, and East Asia had the lowest (17.7%, 478/2702) ([[MA:region]]). The East Asian difference remained after adjustment for publication year, genre, publication type, and specialty (adjusted OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.38-0.51; Table 2) and within journals (OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.40-0.57; Table 3). Reweighting East Asia's specialty mix to match North America and Western Europe raised its rate from 17.7% to 19.1%, compared with 34.7%, closing only 8% of the gap. East Asian rates were also lower in each of the 10 specialties most frequent among East Asian first authors, each with at least 30 records in both groups ([[MA:ea_vs_nawe_within_specialty]]). Within East Asia, rates were 30.1% in Hong Kong, 27.0% in Singapore, and 14.9% in China. These results index first-authored publication discourse, not national attitudes, research quality, or AI capability.

Publication Format
Publication-type analyses used all 14,058 records: Research Articles (n=8556), Reviews (n=4611), Letters (n=429), Editorials (n=364), and Commentaries (n=98). Critical rates were 56.1% (55/98) for Commentaries, 40.6% (174/429) for Letters, 37.1% (135/364) for Editorials, 32.7% (2799/8556) for Research Articles, and 24.3% (1120/4611) for Reviews (Figure 6). Pooling Commentaries, Letters, and Editorials, opinion formats were more critical than Research Articles (40.9%, 364/891, vs 32.7%), and their five-category stance distributions differed (N=9447; χ²₄=376.1; P&lt;.001; Cramér V=0.200) ([[MA:pubtype]]). Letters also had a larger neutral share (21.7%, 93/429) than most other formats.
Reviews were the most favourable format at 73.6% (3395/4611), followed by Research Articles at 61.5%, Editorials at 53.3%, and Commentaries and Letters at 37.8% each. Reviews were therefore both the least critical and the most favourable format. The difference remained after adjustment for publication year, genre, specialty, and first-author region (adjusted OR 0.47, 95% CI 0.41-0.54; average marginal probability 21.5% vs 35.4% for Research Articles; Table 2) and when records were compared within the same journal (OR 0.62, 95% CI 0.50-0.76; Table 3). Systematic reviews were more critical than non-systematic reviews (29.5%, 245/831, vs 23.1%, 875/3780; [[MA:review_subtypes]]). Among critical records, Reviews more often raised governance/regulation (56.9% vs 28.3%) and ethics/bias (44.6% vs 30.5%), and less often hallucination/errors (29.2% vs 52.6%), than Research Articles ([[MA:theme_by_format]]).

Exploratory Forward-Validation of Early Advocacy Predictions
The exploratory forward-validation compared 78 claims from 74 Advocacy papers published in 2021–2023 with the record published from 2024 to April 2026. Two claims (2.6%) were borne out, 54 (69.2%) were partially borne out, 8 (10.3%) were not borne out, and 14 (17.9%) were too early or unfalsifiable ([[MA:forward_validation_sankey]]; [[MA:fv_tier_verdict]]). The analysis was Advocacy-only, single-rater, and retrieval-dependent.
