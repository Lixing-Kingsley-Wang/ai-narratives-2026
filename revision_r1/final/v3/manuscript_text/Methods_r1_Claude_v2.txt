Methods
Study Design
We conducted an LLM-assisted computational content analysis of abstract-level records from Q1/Q2 medical journals published from January 2021 to December 2025. The design was observational and nonexperimental. The unit of analysis was the PubMed record, represented by its title and abstract. The corpus was a census of every eligible record retrieved by the PubMed search; human validation was used to stratify random samples. Reporting followed the applicable elements of the APA Journal Article Reporting Standards for Quantitative Research (JARS-Quant), adapted to a record-level content analysis. [NEW REF: Appelbaum et al 2018, JARS-Quant]. Bibliometric methods constructed the corpus but were not the primary study design. Records were retrieved from PubMed in April 2026.
Ethical Considerations
The study analyzed published titles, abstracts, and bibliographic metadata from PubMed. It involved no human participants and no patient data. Under Article 2.2 of the Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS 2, 2022), research ethics board review is not required for research that relies exclusively on publicly available information that is legally accessible and carries no reasonable expectation of privacy [NEW REF: TCPS 2 2022]. Institutional ethics review was therefore not required. Informed consent and compensation did not apply, because no individuals were recruited or studied. First-author affiliations were used only to assign a country and region, and the shared dataset holds PubMed identifiers and study labels only. The manuscript and its appendices contain no images of identifiable individuals.
Data Sources and Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
We retrieved English-language PubMed/MEDLINE records month by month, by publication date, using AI-technology and medical-context terms ([[MA:note_data_code]]). Retrieval yielded 129,557 records and 116,117 unique PubMed identifiers after deduplication (Figure 1). We excluded 4106 conference proceedings and preprint records. The remaining 112,011 records were matched by normalized journal title to the SCImago Journal Rank (SJR) list for their publication year.
Journals were eligible if they were ranked Q1 or Q2 in any SJR subject category, allowing the inclusion of medical-AI research published in informatics, engineering, and multidisciplinary journals alongside clinical journals. Because this criterion could also admit journals based solely on nonmedical subject rankings, a sensitivity analysis restricted eligibility to journals ranked Q1/Q2 within the SJR Medicine subject area, using narrower and broader definitions to establish bounds ([[MA:sjr_sensitivity]]). Of the 112,011 records, 98,626 (88.1%) were Q1/Q2. A further 7922 were excluded: 5092 were in journals not listed in SJR, 1494 in journals with no quartile, and 1336 in Q4 journals. The 5463 Q3 records formed a comparison arm.

The analytic window was restricted to January 2021 through December 2025 to ensure complete calendar-year coverage. Of the Q1/Q2 records, 11,926 fell outside this window: 10,757 were published from January to April 2026, 1134 had a cover date of May 2026 or later, and 35 were dated before 2021. Records from January to April 2026 were classified using the same pipeline but included only in descriptive analyses ([[MA:update_2026]]). The final eligible corpus is composed of 86,700 records.
AI-Engagement Prefilter
Claude Haiku 4.5 (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001; temperature not set, provider default 1.0) classified each eligible record from its title and the first 300 characters of its abstract. Discourse records discussed AI. Evaluative records tested an AI system and assessed its accuracy, safety, or readiness. Application records used AI only as a method, and the prompt instructed the model to classify uncertain records as application ([[MA:note_prefilter_prompt]]). We retained discourse and evaluative records. Unparseable responses defaulted to be excluded (95 records). The prefilter retained 14,066 records: 7637 discourse and 6429 evaluative. For the validation audit, the same model and prompt reclassified all eligible records from the title and full abstract. The annual keep rate, the proportion of eligible records retained, is reported for each year ([[MA:keep_rate]]).
Unit of Analysis Characteristics
Each record carried its publication year, PubMed publication type, journal, and first-author affiliation. Classification added a prefilter genre (discourse or evaluative), a stance label, and a specialty or domain category, and critical records also received concern themes and, when an abstract was present, failure-mechanism and model-type labels. The distribution of the analytic corpus across these characteristics is reported in the Results.
Sampling Procedures
The corpus was not sampled. Every eligible record entered the prefilter, and every retained record entered stance classification. Sampling was used only to select records for human validation. Each validation sample was drawn by stratified or simple random sampling with a fixed seed, and its design is described under Quality of Measurements (Table 1).
Sample Size, Power, and Precision
Sample size was set by the census, so no power calculation was performed. We report precision instead. The 95% CI half-width was 0.8 percentage points for the overall critical share, and from 1.2 (2025) to 2.6 (2021) percentage points for annual critical shares ([[MA:precision]]). Validation sample sizes were fixed before all coding.
Measures and Covariates
Evaluative Stance
Claude Sonnet 4.6 (claude-sonnet-4-6; Batch API; temperature not set, provider default 1.0) classified each retained record from its title and full abstract with the canonical v1 prompt ([[MA:note_stance_prompt_v1]]). Alarm marked abstracts that were primarily critical or skeptical of AI, emphasizing dangers, failures, hallucinations, safety risks, ethical problems, or unreadiness for use; papers showing that AI performed worse than clinicians were also coded Alarm. Caution marked abstracts that acknowledged both promise and significant concerns, with the balance tilting toward concern and safeguards recommended before deployment. Neutral marked purely descriptive abstracts that reported metrics without an evaluative position. Cautious Optimism marked broadly positive abstracts with caveats that supported careful deployment. Advocacy marked strongly pro-AI abstracts that emphasized transformative potential with minimal caveats. The prompt also coded papers whose main topic was AI risk, ethics, or governance as Alarm or Caution, and told the model not to default to Cautious Optimism. Critical stance was Alarm plus Caution; favourable stance was Cautious Optimism plus Advocacy. Of the 14,066 retained records after the prefilter, 8 returned unparseable responses, leaving 14,058 records in the analytic corpus. Two re-inferences of the 300 validation records agreed with the original labels in 95.3% of records (quadratic-weighted κ=0.97), and all changes were between adjacent categories ([[MA:run_to_run]]).
The two ends of the scale are not defined symmetrically. The prompt codes plain reports of AI underperformance as Alarm, but it has no matching rule for plain reports of AI outperformance, which reach Advocacy only with an emphasis on transformative potential. A sensitivity analysis tested this design. Claude Sonnet 4.6 (temperature 0) reclassified every favourable record for strong endorsement, defined as a strongly positive conclusion with minimal or no caveats, whatever its wording ([[MA:note_endorsement_prompt]]). Strong endorsement then served as the favourable counterpart of Alarm. In a separate sensitivity analysis, a stricter, conclusion-anchored v2 prompt reclassified all records ([[MA:note_stance_prompt_v2]]). All reported stance labels come from v1.
Concern Themes
Among the 4283 critical records, Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Batch API; temperature not set) returned the one to three most prominent concern themes from the title and the first 400 characters of the abstract. The ten themes were patient safety, hallucination/errors, governance/regulation, ethics/bias, medical education, data privacy, cognitive offloading, existential threat, replacement, and other ([[MA:note_theme_prompt]]). 
Failure Mechanism and Model Type
Because the hallucination theme covered both inaccurate and fabricated output, Claude Sonnet 4.6 (synchronous API with concurrency 5; temperature 0) classified each critical record on two axes from its title and full abstract ([[MA:note_mechanism_prompt]]). Failure mode was confabulation, misclassification, both, or none or unclear; we report confabulation as fabrication and misclassification as factual error. Model type was generative, discriminative, both, or unclear. Of the 4283 critical records, 4281 were parsed and 3906 had a full abstract. Mechanism analyses used these 3906 records and the hallucination-gated subset, the 1775 among them that also carried the hallucination theme.
Specialty and Domain
Claude Haiku 4.5 (claude-haiku-4-5; Batch API; temperature not set) assigned each record one of 18 specialty or domain categories from its title and abstract, based on clinical domain ([[MA:note_specialty_prompt]]; [[MA:specialty_definitions]]). Ten records could not be classified. Primary analyses excluded categories with fewer than 30 records.
First-Author Country and Region
The first author and affiliation came from PubMed XML document order, without corresponding-author logic. Two rule-based parsers, without an LLM, read the affiliation. A country parser matched explicit country names and resolved 12,745 of 14,058 records (90.7%); it was used for country estimates and maps. A region parser assigned one of eight regions or Unknown; 13,554 records (96.4%) had a known region ([[MA:note_data_code]]). Country maps required at least 50 records per country, with a 20-record sensitivity map ([[MA:country_map_20]]).
Publication Type and Review Subtype
PubMed Publication Type metadata were collapsed into five categories, Research Article, Review, Letter, Editorial, and Commentary, by a fixed precedence rule ([[MA:note_data_code]]). Reviews tagged Systematic Review in PubMed were classed as systematic reviews, and all other reviews, including scoping reviews, as non-systematic reviews.
FDA Cleared-Device Count
Cleared-device availability used the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) list of AI-enabled medical devices, comprising 1430 devices cleared from 1995 to 2025 [17]. Lead review panels were mapped to the 18 categories ([[MA:fda_mapping]]). The count is a US measure of cleared-device availability, not of adoption or use, and it entered only an exploratory ecological analysis. Generative share was the proportion of each category's abstract-present critical records classified as generative or both.
Forward-Validation
An exploratory analysis checked the predictive claims of 2021–2023 Advocacy records against evidence published from 2024 to April 2026. Its design is in [[MA:note_forward_validation]], and its results are in [[MA:forward_validation_sankey]] and [[MA:fv_tier_verdict]].
Quality of Measurements
Every LLM classification step was validated against human coding (Table 1). One author (LW) coded every validation sample, blind to the model labels and sampling strata. Agreement is reported as Cohen κ, quadratic-weighted for the ordered stance scale and unweighted for nominal labels, with percentile bootstrap 95% CIs (1000 resamples, seed 42) unless stated otherwise.
Prefilter
A 200-record development pilot preceded the production run. It had no written codebook, and its sampling was not documented; raw agreement was 85.5% (171/200), and Cohen κ was 0.511 (95% CI 0.345-0.646) ([[MA:prefilter_validation]]). We then audited the production prefilter. Records were stratified by prefilter outcome and year (2021–2025). Excluded records were split by the full-abstract reclassification into those it newly kept (75 sampled) and those it also excluded (225 sampled). Fifty retained (kept by both) records were also sampled, 20 pilot records were recoded to estimate intra-rater agreement during the audit. The coder read the title and full abstract of all 370 records in random order. Estimates were weighted to stratum sizes. The primary estimands were sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, and κ for the production prefilter, and the false-negative rate among excluded records by year, with the implied number of missed records. Intervals for weighted estimates came from 10,000 draws of the Jeffreys posterior of each stratum-year cell. Sensitivity was 78.5% (95% CI 66.8%-81.7%), specificity was 99.5% (95% CI 97.0%-99.6%), and Cohen κ was 0.842 (95% CI 0.713-0.856).
Stance
Stance validation used 300 records with an abstract, stratified by model stance (Alarm 40, Caution 60, Neutral 70, Cautious Optimism 100, and Advocacy 30). The primary analysis used the 225 records the coder judged codable and in scope. Quadratic-weighted κ was 0.79 (95% CI 0.72-0.84) in scope and 0.75 (95% CI 0.68-0.81) across all 300 records. Per-class precision, recall, and the confusion matrix are in [[MA:stance_confusion]]. For Advocacy, the classifier labelled 17 of the 24 records that the coder had labelled Advocacy, and 17 of its 26 Advocacy labels matched the coder. All disagreements within scope were between Advocacy and Cautious Optimism.
Because Advocacy is rare, we also estimated misclassification-corrected annual prevalence of Advocacy and of critical stance. For each year, the share of records in each model category was multiplied by the share of that category the coder assigned to the target label, and the products were summed ([[MA:note_correction_estimator]]). The primary correction assumes that these conversion rates were constant across years. Because the validation sample was not stratified by year, a post hoc analysis estimated the rates separately for 2021–2023 and 2024–2025 ([[MA:period_correction]]).
Concern Themes
Theme validation used 200 critical records, 40 per year from 2021 to 2025, drawn by simple random sampling within year. The coder read the title and full abstract and coded one to three most prominent themes. We report per-theme precision, recall, and F1, with micro- and macro-F1, exact match, Hamming loss, and Jaccard similarity ([[MA:theme_validation]]). Micro-F1 was 0.885 (95% CI 0.855-0.914), and F1 for the four headline themes ranged from 0.88 to 0.92.
Mechanism and Specialty
Mechanism validation used 120 critical records stratified by year and model failure mode, and the 108 with an abstract were scored. Cohen κ was 0.84 (95% CI 0.74-0.93) for model type and 0.57 (95% CI 0.46-0.69) for failure mode. A nonblind review of disagreements found that the classifier assigned fabrication more readily than the coder, so fabrication estimates can be interpreted as an upper bound ([[MA:mechanism_confusion]]). Specialty validation used 120 records stratified by model category, with an option to mark a record as unclear from the abstract; Cohen κ was 0.75 (95% CI 0.66-0.83).
Missing Data
Each analysis used the records with a valid value for the variables it involved, and no missing values were imputed ([[MA:missing_data]]). Failed responses were rare: 95 in the prefilter, which defaulted for exclusion, 8 for stance, 2 for mechanism, and 10 for specialty. Title-only records (892, 6.3%) were kept in every analysis except the mechanism analyses. First-author country was unresolved by the non-LLM parser for 1313 records (9.3%), and region was Unknown for 504 (3.6%); Unknown region was kept as its own category in regressions. PubMed records without a publication month are stored as January, so January held 16% to 19% of each year's records, and we did not analyze months or quarters.
Analytic Strategy
Proportions are reported with n/N and 95% percentile bootstrap CIs (1000 resamples, seed 42, with a fixed seed for each cell). For cells with no events, exact Clopper–Pearson intervals are also given.
Each temporal trend was tested with a record-level logistic regression of the outcome on publication year, reported as the odds ratio (OR) per year with a 95% CI from journal cluster-robust standard errors (Figure 2; [[MA:stance_trend]] and [[MA:themes]]). Spearman ρ across the five annual values, with exact permutation P values, is reported as a descriptive summary; with five points, the smallest two-sided P value it can reach is .017.
For critical stance, a multivariable logistic model adjusted the year trend for prefilter genre, publication type, specialty, and first-author region, with standard errors clustered by journal (2278 journals). Reference levels were the most frequent categories: discourse, Research Article, Medical Informatics/Digital Health, and North America. We report adjusted odds ratios (ORs) and predicted probabilities averaged across all records (Table 2). We used the same predictors to examine Advocacy. To assess the robustness of the findings, we repeated the analysis with year as a categorical variable, included records from January–April 2026, replaced first-author region with country, and accounted for differences between journals using fixed effects. The journal fixed-effects analysis was restricted to 275 journals with at least 10 records and variation in the outcome ([[MA:regression_full]]). We also tested an ordinal logistic model using all five stance categories. However, the model's assumption that predictors have consistent effects across stance thresholds was violated for 27 of 32 terms, particularly when separating Advocacy from the other categories. We therefore retained the binary logistic models as the primary analysis. Across the 18 categories, critical rate was compared with the FDA count using Spearman correlation, with the two-sided P value as primary. The analysis was repeated without the 7 structural-zero categories and as a partial correlation controlling for generative share ([[MA:fda_robustness]]). East Asian first-author rates were reweighted to the specialty mix of North American and Western European first authors and compared within specialty ([[MA:ea_vs_nawe_within_specialty]]). Stance distributions of opinion formats (Commentary, Letter, and Editorial) and Research Articles were compared with a χ² test and Cramér V.
Sensitivity analyses addressed the prefilter, the stance scale, and the journal set. For the prefilter, excluded records that the full-abstract reclassification kept were given v1 stance labels (2453 records dated 2021–2025), and trends were re-estimated in the augmented corpus and in a corpus defined by the full-abstract labels ([[MA:corpus_sensitivity]]). For the stance scale, trends were re-estimated with the v2 rubric and the strong-endorsement scale ([[MA:stance_trend]] and [[MA:symmetric_endorsement]]). For the journal set, main estimates were repeated in the SJR Medicine corpus and in the Q3 comparison arm ([[MA:sjr_sensitivity]] and [[MA:q3_arm]]). Critical share was also estimated within each prefilter genre ([[MA:within_genre]]).
To check the LLM themes against an independent source, we refetched MeSH descriptors and author keywords for the retained records from PubMed ([[MA:note_mesh_keywords]]). Three prespecified analyses followed. A fixed lexicon for each headline theme counted the annual share of critical records that mentioned the theme in the title, abstract, author keywords, or MeSH, for comparison with the LLM theme trend. We used weighted log-odds with an informative Dirichlet prior to identify terms that became more prominent between 2021–2022 and 2024–2025, and terms that differed most between critical and favourable records. This approach accounts for differences in word frequency and reduces the influence of rare terms ([[MA:lexicon_vs_llm_fig]] and [[MA:terms_fig]]; [[MA:lexicon_and_terms]]).
Statistical tests were ran two-sided at α=.05. Analyses were descriptive only and hypothesis-generating; P values were not adjusted for multiplicity, and interpretation emphasized effect sizes and CIs. Python 3.12 and statsmodels 0.15.0 was used for analysis, with package versions pinned in the repository [18]. Prompts, code, PubMed identifiers, and committed outputs are available there. Because hosted-model behaviour drifts, the committed outputs are the basis for exact reproduction.
Use of Large Language Models
Claude Haiku 4.5 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic) were the study's measurement instruments (Table 1). Haiku 4.5 performed the prefilter and specialty classification. Sonnet 4.6 performed stance, theme, mechanism, and strong-endorsement classification, and extracted and summarized claims in the forward-validation. Only their outputs entered the analyses. Each output used as data came from a scripted run, was checked for schema validity, and was used only within its defined analytic role. Claude Opus 4.6 assisted with code review, drafting, and editing for the original submission. For this revision, Sonnet 5.5 in Claude Code assisted with writing and running analysis code, and Opus 5.5 assisted with drafting and editing text. These models produced no data used in the analyses. The authors checked all code and outputs and take responsibility for the content.
